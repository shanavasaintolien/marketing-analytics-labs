# 📊 Lab 1: Data & Storytelling in Marketing Analytics

**Course:** Marketing Analytics  
**Week:** 1  
**Submission Location:** Canvas  
**Lab Type:** Individual  
**Estimated Time:** 60–90 minutes

---

## 🎯 Lab Overview

In marketing analytics, data does not speak for itself — **people tell stories with data**.

In this lab, you will explore how the **same dataset** can be used to:
- Tell a **clear, ethical, and insightful story**, OR
- Tell a **misleading, incomplete, or biased story**

This lab focuses on **critical thinking, communication, and ethical reasoning**, not technical tools.

No prior analytics experience is required.

---

## 🧠 Learning Objectives

By completing this lab, you will be able to:
- Interpret basic marketing performance data
- Understand how data framing influences decision-making
- Identify ethical and unethical uses of data
- Communicate insights clearly to a non-technical audience

---

## 📁 Files Provided

- `dataset.csv` – Marketing campaign performance data
- (Optional) `scenario-context.pdf` – Business context for the dataset

---

## 📊 Dataset Description

The dataset represents performance metrics from four marketing channels:
- Instagram Ads
- Google Ads
- Email Marketing
- Influencer Marketing

Metrics include:
- Spend
- Clicks
- Conversions
- Conversion Rate

You may **not** change the data values.

---

## ✏️ Your Tasks

### **Part A: Tell a “Good” Data Story**
Using the dataset, create a clear and ethical story that answers:

- What is working well?
- Which channel(s) appear most effective?
- What recommendation would you make to a marketing team?

Your goal is to tell a story that is:
- Honest
- Well-reasoned
- Supported by the data

---

### **Part B: Tell a “Bad” or Misleading Data Story**
Using the **same dataset**, tell a story that is misleading.

You may:
- Focus on only one metric
- Ignore important context
- Frame the data to support a weak conclusion

Your goal is to demonstrate how data can be manipulated **without changing the numbers**.

---

### **Part C: Reflection**
Answer the following questions:

1. Why is data storytelling powerful in marketing?
2. What ethical risks exist when presenting data?
3. How can analysts protect against misleading interpretations?
