# 📊 Lab 01: Asking Better Research Questions❓

## 🎯 Learning Objective
In this lab, you will work in groups to practice crafting strong marketing research questions that guide meaningful analysis. 

Before data can be collected or analyzed, marketers must clearly define the problem or opportunity and determine what information is needed to support decision-making.

 To move from a vague situation to actionable insights, researchers typically follow a problem formulation process, which includes:
1. Meeting with clients or stakeholders
2. Clarifying the problem or opportunity
3. Stating the manager’s decision problem
4. Developing research questions
5. Selecting research problems
6. Preparing a research request agreement


In this lab, you will focus on the middle of this process: clarifying the problem and developing research questions.

## Pricing Overview

Typical menu price ranges include:
| Item Category                                        | Typical Price Range |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |

| Smoothies and fresh juices                           | $5 – $8             |
| Sides and add-ons (extra protein, avocado, toppings) | $1.50 – $4          |


Healthy fast-casual chains with similar menus often price their bowls in the $13–$17 range, with premium options reaching around $18–$21 depending on ingredients.

Delivery orders often cost more than in-store purchases due to:
- delivery platform fees
- service charges
- tips
- higher menu prices on delivery apps

Delivery orders are typically $4-$6 higher than if purchased in stores (on average).

## Part 2: Manager Decision Problem
The manager’s decision problem focuses on what decision the company needs to make.
Examples of decision problems might include (do not use any of these):
Should the company invest more in delivery services?
Should the company redesign the in-store experience?
Should the company adjust pricing or promotions?

📌 Write **one clear statement** describing the manager’s decision problem.
Example format:
The marketing manager needs to decide whether…

## Part 3: Research Questions
Now that the situation and decision problem are clearer, your task is to develop research questions that would help inform the decision.
Good research questions should:
- Focus on understanding customer behavior
- Identify possible causes or drivers
- Lead to information that can be analyzed with data

📌 Write **3-4 research questions** that would help the company better understand the situation.
Examples of strong research questions might explore (but are not limited to):
- Customer preferences
- Ordering behavior
- Competitor comparisons

⚠️ Avoid yes/no questions. Focus on questions that require data and analysis.
